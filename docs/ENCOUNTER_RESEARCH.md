# Encounter design research and adopted rules

Research conducted 2026-10-23 after the initial companion, composed-group and reward implementation. These are adaptations for Roag' open world and party combat, not claims that another game's algorithms have been reproduced. The initial implementation has three compositions; the following pass extends their selection, variety and behavior.

## Primary-source findings

- **Slay the Spire:** Anthony Giovannetti explains why unpredictable enemy actions made it difficult to choose a strategy, and how the team moved from verbose inspection to visible icons and numbers. He also describes combining playtest feedback and recorded data when balancing. Adopt readable saved plans and reproducible measurements; do not replace this game's party abilities with a deck. [Nintendo developer interview, 2019](https://www.nintendo.com/jp/topics/article/1f6d2f1e-b7ea-11e9-b641-063b7ac45a6d).
- **Caves of Qud:** C. Brian Bucklew's technical guide distinguishes older encounter tables from zone templates plus population tables, including dynamic membership and explicit exclusions. His linked developer discussion explains selecting a complete encounter versus one item from a table. These are historical descriptions, not an assertion of the exact 2026 implementation. Adopt separate contextual selection and deliberately composed groups; retain explicit reference validation instead of implicit inclusion of every new character. [Technical guide, 2019](https://freehold.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/CQP/pages/25690114), [developer discussion, 2016](https://steamcommunity.com/app/333640/discussions/3/358415738194955181/).
- **Dungeon Crawl Stone Soup:** Its public source chooses populations by branch and depth with positional habitat vetoes; a separate system chooses bands, members and leaders. Adopt context eligibility and whole-group placement on suitable, connected ground. [Population selection](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crawl/crawl/master/crawl-ref/source/mon-pick.cc), [population data](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crawl/crawl/master/crawl-ref/source/mon-pick-data.h), [band placement](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crawl/crawl/master/crawl-ref/source/mon-place.cc).
- **Dungeon Crawl's design manual:** Transparent rules, meaningful decisions, varied challenges and avoiding profitable repetition are explicit goals. Authored vaults combine themes, threats and loot; unusual threats can also occur early. Adopt finite, persistent encounter identities and one-time rewards, with fixed dangers and freely accessible locations. This does not imply guaranteeing victory for any party. [Official manual, design philosophy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crawl/crawl/master/crawl-ref/docs/crawl_manual.rst).
- **Darkest Dungeon:** Red Hook presents health, stress and expedition recovery as intertwined concerns and describes camping to heal or encourage companions. Adopt groups that pressure different existing resources—health, mana, medicine and stress—so target priority, preparation and recovery matter. No new positional ranks or affliction system are introduced here. [Red Hook overview](https://www.darkestdungeon.com/darkest-dungeon/about/).
- **Slay the Spire 2:** Mega Crit's June 2026 update describes a bestiary exposing encountered enemies' moves and animations. Adopt discoverable, illustrated combat information through the existing hover/intention/Tactics flow. A separate collectible bestiary is outside this milestone. [Mega Crit update](https://megacrit.com/news/2026-6-19-neowsletter-issue-23/).

The GDC Vault balance talk and Qud presentation URLs redirected to a maintenance page during this research. Their unviewed contents are not used as evidence. Community guides and search snippets suggested leads, but the decisions above rely on developer accounts, official documentation or public project source.

## Local design decisions

These are our design inferences, rather than rules attributed verbatim to the sources.

1. Encounter JSON describes a complete compatible composition, its roles, tactical explanation and reward. Separate eligibility fields select by site purpose, regional biome and depth. Weighted variation changes which coherent group appears; it never assembles unrestricted combinations of healers or charging attackers.
2. Each underground site floor receives a bounded group. Avoid repeating the same composition inside a site when eligible alternatives exist. Composition, identity and placement use independent seeded randomness. Party level and visit order cannot strengthen, reroll or respawn a group. The final invariant review also removed inherited party-size scaling from ambient nearby-foe selection; the shared opponent query now drives battle membership and visible hover estimates.
3. Groups need connected traversable cells, distinct occupancy and room around stairs/caches. Placement must not carve arbitrary passageways or discover terrain. Generation/audit reports must expose unsatisfied placement or content coverage instead of hiding it.
4. Patrols, warbands and elites have different authored compositions and reward profiles. These names communicate authored risk; the numerical hover estimate remains relative to the current party. Depth influences populations but never locks access.
5. Preparations show their future action before release. Stun breaks the preparation, including after a later cleanse; a fallen fixed target wastes the release. Support targets wounded allies. Limited mana and authored non-repeat rules prevent one behavior from dominating indefinitely. Plans never silently change after the player has seen them.
6. Thematic dungeon caches and encounter rewards share regional/guardian equipment identity; boss signature items come from victories. Persistent sources pay once and seeded items survive saves, retreat and equipping without duplication.
7. Audit structural variety separately from combat outcomes. Reports must include composition frequencies, context coverage, placement and continuation checks, plus real comparisons of appropriate counterplay. Scripted success is not proof that balance or long journeys are enjoyable; fresh-run playtesting remains necessary.

## Review boundaries

Keep generated companions, four-person turn-based combat, paused combat time, shared simulation rules, mouse controls, the 4096×4096 world, outdoor visibility and dungeon fog. Do not introduce card decks, mandatory level gates, endless spawning, party-scaled enemies, a strategic colony layer or a new battle grid. Current authored stats are an initial tuning baseline; reports should make later adjustments easy and reviewable.
