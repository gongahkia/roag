# Dullest Dungeon: the Company of Necessary Copies

Dullest Dungeon is the little painted game box on a table in Jomon's tavern. Its ridiculous premise is that four office workers cross a floor of desks, steal a rival department's file, and carry it back for approval. To Jomon's sailors, who know storms, blades, and difficult roads, this invented land of forms and chairs is exotic fantasy. The box contains a folding floor plan, two wooden file tokens, four worker tokens per side, coffee and staples, and a large stack of cards. Nobody in Jomon has a real office, computer, or printer because this is a fictional setting inside their game.

This is original Jomon fiction informed by historical objects, not a claim that modern offices existed in Jomon's world. The idea of a hand-painted, socially shared deck draws on surviving late-medieval European playing cards, which depicted their own casts and worlds in art. [The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2016/world-in-play) documents those cards. The game's configurable partitions and competing floor plans take a cue from Herman Miller's [Action Office history](https://www.hermanmiller.com/products/workspaces/workstations/action-office-system/design-story/). Its files, retention policies, and absurd approval ritual exaggerate real records-scheduling language described by the [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling). The more improbable cards borrow visual ideas from the history of [punched cards and office automation](https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/story/2), the [Library of Congress office-equipment guide](https://guides.loc.gov/office-history), and [OSHA's workstation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/workstation-environment). None of those sources supply the game's fictional characters or rules.

## The company's impossible history

According to the box's deliberately contradictory rulebook, the Company of Necessary Copies was founded when the First Clerk made two identical files and neither could prove which was original. The Board solved the dispute by declaring both files official, then requiring each department to borrow the other department's copy before approving its own. This circular order created an entire economy of signatures. The company's oldest prophecy is a blank form stamped **RECEIVED**. Its newest prophecy is the same form stamped **PLEASE RESUBMIT**.

Every match is a new floor of the company's headquarters. Its eleven departments are Records Retention, Human Resources, Facilities, Accounts Payable, the Mailroom, the Breakroom, Compliance, Procurement, Information Technology, the Boardroom, and the Basement. The game insists that the Basement is above the Boardroom on alternate Thursdays. Six mirrored furniture plans represent successive reorganizations. Nobody agrees whether the walls are desks, cubicles, or defensive paperwork, but they always have the same effect on movement. The central corridor remains open so both departments can reach each other's file.

Workers are jobs, not Jomon people. A courier chooses four from twenty-five archetypes: Middle Manager, Facilities Engineer, Wellness Officer, Mailroom Runner, Meeting Scheduler, Consultant, Procurement Clerk, Compliance Agent, Breakroom Botanist, Office Printer, Corporate Lawyer, Presentation Lead, Morale Officer, Spreadsheet Analyst, Elevator Operator, Climate Technician, Office Gardener, Copier Mechanic, Budget Controller, Mold Inspector, Basement Archivist, Outage Coordinator, Records Clerk, Remote Worker, and Ergonomics Rep. These titles give an otherwise mundane action an absurdly high-stakes voice. The Office Printer's player must explain why a machine holds an employment contract. The Remote Worker is always represented by a token, even when its card claims it is absent.

The game's 290 action cards are organized around those jobs. A card may move a worker, add cover, apply paperwork pressure, restore composure, draw cards, change energy, or impose a condition such as being flagged, frazzled, under review, or held in a meeting. Some cards do several things in order. Fifty two-branch masteries allow a more forceful effect or a longer reach; sixteen card treatments change a copy's handling; eleven company policies change a team's style. These are rules of the tabletop game alone. They do not alter Jomon's real combat.

## A match at the table

The board is 41 by 15 desks. Each side has four workers, a shared deck of 20–30 cards, a five-card hand, and three energy per turn. There are two file tokens. To score, a worker picks up the rival's file, returns near its own file tray while its own file is home, and keeps the stolen file through the rival's next turn. The file is then signed and returned. A knocked-out carrier drops its file; the worker comes back after two turns of mandatory leave. Coffee grants energy and staples provide cover and a draw. All travel is caused by cards. The always available **Commute** costs one energy and moves one desk, so a bad hand cannot strand a team. First to two approvals wins. Otherwise the higher score after eighteen rounds wins; a tie receives four overtime rounds and may end in a draw.

The patron is a named adult presently in Jomon's tavern. They play the same card and movement rules under a deterministic local policy. The match is private and offline; there is no network opponent or second campaign. Starting a match costs one Jomon action. Choosing cards, moving tokens, and waiting at the table do not advance Jomon's world clock. The current match, deck order, random state, and event queue are stored inside Jomon's atomic save. If a courier dies, that courier's card collection remains archived under their identity. Another living courier starts with their own collection.

Jomon's tavern regulars argue about the game as if they had held the jobs themselves. One calls the Office Printer a beast of burden; another claims it is the only honest worker in the Company. A first victory over a particular patron in a season earns one Jomon trade credit, one bounded improvement in that patron's opinion, and one point of the courier's recorded Strategy, up to twenty. Strategy is a record of play and has no effect on other Jomon systems. Repeat victories that season earn only the result in the ledger. There is no claim that the fictional Company exists outside the box.

For the competitive handoff between an ensemble game and a distinct match, the design also considered Supergiant's explanation of [Pyre's separate versus mode](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/pyre-introducing-head-to-head-versus-mode/). Dullest Dungeon is deliberately local patron-versus-courier play inside Jomon rather than a new main loop.

## Implementation boundary

The imported `dumbest_dungeon` source and its catalog now live in the Jomon repository. `office_content.py` gives the 25 roles and 290 cards their office faces; `tabletop.py` supplies spatial competitive rules and uses the imported event queue; `tabletop_ui.py` runs inside Jomon's existing curses screen. The imported PvE expedition modules remain as source history but are not a second playable campaign. Running `python -m dumbest_dungeon` opens Jomon. The original Dullest Dungeon save format is not loaded or copied; only Jomon's format-8 parent save stores the current table match and per-courier collections. Format-7 Jomon saves migrate by adding an empty table ledger and zero Strategy without changing their Jomon possessions or geography.

Run `python3 -m jomon.checks --pattern test_dullest_dungeon.py` for table rules and save boundaries, then `python3 -m jomon.checks full` for the whole Jomon suite.
