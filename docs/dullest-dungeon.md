# Dullest Dungeon: the Company of Necessary Copies

Dullest Dungeon is the painted game box on a table in Jomon's tavern. Its ridiculous premise is that four office workers cross a generated office dungeon, steal a rival department's file, and carry it back for approval. To Jomon's sailors, who know storms, blades, and difficult roads, this invented land of forms and chairs is exotic fantasy. The box contains two file tokens, one party token per side, specialist portraits, and a large stack of cards. Nobody in Jomon has a real office, computer, or printer because this is a fictional setting inside their game.

This is original Jomon fiction informed by historical objects, not a claim that modern offices existed in Jomon's world. The idea of a hand-painted, socially shared deck draws on surviving late-medieval European playing cards, which depicted their own casts and worlds in art. [The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2016/world-in-play) documents those cards. The game's configurable partitions and competing floor plans take a cue from Herman Miller's [Action Office history](https://www.hermanmiller.com/products/workspaces/workstations/action-office-system/design-story/). Its files, retention policies, and absurd approval ritual exaggerate real records-scheduling language described by the [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling). The more improbable cards borrow visual ideas from the history of [punched cards and office automation](https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/story/2), the [Library of Congress office-equipment guide](https://guides.loc.gov/office-history), and [OSHA's workstation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/workstation-environment). None of those sources supply the game's fictional characters or rules.

## The company's impossible history

According to the box's deliberately contradictory rulebook, the Company of Necessary Copies was founded when the First Clerk made two identical files and neither could prove which was original. The Board solved the dispute by declaring both files official, then requiring each department to borrow the other department's copy before approving its own. This circular order created an entire economy of signatures. The company's oldest prophecy is a blank form stamped **RECEIVED**. Its newest prophecy is the same form stamped **PLEASE RESUBMIT**.

Every match is a new floor of the company's headquarters. Eleven departments and six generated layouts represent successive reorganizations. Nobody agrees whether the walls are desks, cubicles, or defensive paperwork, but the original expedition's terrain costs still govern movement. Both records offices are connected by traversable routes. Neutral hazards, service desks, supply caches, rest rooms, workshops, and event rooms remain; scripted enemy patrols, access objectives, and bosses have been replaced by the rival party and two files.

Workers are jobs, not Jomon people. A courier chooses four from twenty-five archetypes: Middle Manager, Facilities Engineer, Wellness Officer, Mailroom Runner, Meeting Scheduler, Consultant, Procurement Clerk, Compliance Agent, Breakroom Botanist, Office Printer, Corporate Lawyer, Presentation Lead, Morale Officer, Spreadsheet Analyst, Elevator Operator, Climate Technician, Office Gardener, Copier Mechanic, Budget Controller, Mold Inspector, Basement Archivist, Outage Coordinator, Records Clerk, Remote Worker, and Ergonomics Rep. These titles give an otherwise mundane action an absurdly high-stakes voice. The Office Printer's player must explain why a machine holds an employment contract. The Remote Worker is always represented by a token, even when its card claims it is absent.

The lobby lets a courier browse all twenty-five jobs, choose one of thirteen original curated formation plans under new company names, and switch each worker between a starter and alternate five-card package. The deck editor still permits custom 20–30-card combinations. Every job has its own office-fantasy ASCII portrait and card glyph. The company archive displays the worker and technique catalogs, all six floorplans and eleven departments, company policies, the courier's match ledger, and the original 129 enemy drawings reinterpreted as rival-department costumes. Costumes change only the patron party's appearance; the wearer uses its selected specialist's cards and rules, not the original scripted enemy action.

The game's 290 action cards are organized around those jobs. A card may move a worker, add cover, apply paperwork pressure, restore composure, draw cards, change energy, or impose a condition such as being flagged, frazzled, under review, or held in a meeting. Some cards do several things in order. Fifty two-branch masteries allow a more forceful effect or a longer reach; sixteen card treatments change a copy's handling; eleven company policies change a team's style. These are rules of the tabletop game alone. They do not alter Jomon's real combat.

## A match at the table

The dungeon is 117 by 35 cells, generated by the imported expedition rules. Each side moves as one party token, with up to four destination orders of at most eighteen weighted travel ticks per turn. Travel consumes light at the imported terrain-cost rate; low light and travel-linked liabilities add stress. Contact opens ranked card combat: each side has four specialists, a shared deck of 20–30 cards, a five-card opening hand, and three energy per turn. Both sides choose legal origin ranks, card targets, and effects under the same rules. Combat lasts until a party is wiped or retreats to a neighboring floor tile away from the rival; retreat forfeits the rest of that side's movement for the turn. Health and conditions persist. A knocked-out specialist remains out of the current fight, then returns after two of that side's map turns of mandatory leave. Neutral caches add cards or persistent perks; rest rooms recover or treat a liability; workshops upgrade a specific card copy; events, service desks, and hazards affect the party that encounters them.

To score, a living specialist carries the rival file within two cells of its own home while its own file is home, then holds through the rival's next turn. The file is signed and returned. A knocked-out carrier drops the file. First to two approvals wins. Otherwise the higher score after eighteen map rounds wins; a tie receives four overtime map rounds and may end in a draw. Combat exchanges pause this clock.

The patron is a named adult presently in Jomon's tavern. They use the same route, deck, rank, energy, targeting, and card actions under a deterministic local policy. The match is private and offline; there is no network opponent or second campaign. Starting a match costs one Jomon action. Choosing cards, moving tokens, and waiting at the table do not advance Jomon's world clock. The current match, deck order, and random state are stored inside Jomon's atomic save. If a courier dies, that courier's card collection remains archived under their identity. Another living courier starts with their own collection.

Jomon's tavern regulars argue about the game as if they had held the jobs themselves. One calls the Office Printer a beast of burden; another claims it is the only honest worker in the Company. A first victory over a particular patron in a season earns one Jomon trade credit, one bounded improvement in that patron's opinion, and one point of the courier's recorded Strategy, up to twenty. Strategy is a record of play and has no effect on other Jomon systems. Repeat victories that season earn only the result in the ledger. There is no claim that the fictional Company exists outside the box.

For the competitive handoff between an ensemble game and a distinct match, the design also considered Supergiant's explanation of [Pyre's separate versus mode](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/pyre-introducing-head-to-head-versus-mode/). Dullest Dungeon is deliberately local patron-versus-courier play inside Jomon rather than a new main loop.

## Implementation boundary

The imported `dumbest_dungeon` generator and catalog remain in the Jomon repository. `expedition.py` uses them for the competitive match; `expedition_ui.py` presents the generated map, bespoke office portraits, rival costumes, and original-proportion ranked cards inside Jomon's curses screen. `office_content.py` provides the office-fantasy names. The retired small-board reducer and the unused standalone PvE screen have been removed from Jomon; the sibling repository still contains the standalone campaign. The imported PvE expedition is not a second playable mode. Running `python -m dumbest_dungeon` opens Jomon. Standalone Dullest Dungeon saves are not loaded. Jomon format-9 saves store current expeditions; migration from format 8 discards the superseded small-board game state while preserving other Jomon progress.

Run `python3 -m jomon.checks --pattern test_dullest_dungeon.py` for table rules and save boundaries, then `python3 -m jomon.checks full` for the whole Jomon suite.
