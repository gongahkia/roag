# Finite procedural world

The world client highlights the cursor cell and caches a discovered-ground route preview until the cell, origin, discovery, edits or session changes. Selected routes remain visible during travel. Clicking reachable ground starts travel, including while paused. Visible people, caches, storage and known stairs use observed approach routes and automatic interaction on arrival. Moving actors are hit-tested at their interpolated visible sprite positions; the selector stays on the cursor cell. Hovering never submits a command. On-screen controls cover all world actions and combat; keyboard gameplay input is disabled. Panels and disabled controls intercept clicks before map routing. Presentation interpolation follows committed party/NPC coordinates with the camera, preserves corners and horizontal facing, and resets on floor/session changes or long transfers. It does not change authoritative collision, costs, simulation ticks, random state or save coordinates. Original sprite crops are flipped only while drawing; source artwork remains unchanged.

The client supplies its local surface bounds to `WorldSession.RevealSurface`: the whole view is visible without wall occlusion, and observed cells join persistent discovery. Bounds are cached until the camera view changes and retained across movement/terrain refreshes. Dungeon entry drops those surface bounds and retains radius-11 line-of-sight visibility, dim remembered terrain and discovered-only routes. No distant or underground cells are exposed by surface observation. The world map still uses persistent discovery. The 4096×4096 world is unchanged. Exploration defaults to 36–64 pixels per tile and zoom never goes below 24 pixels or beyond 48 columns / 32 rows; the separate world map supplies the overview. This also stays within the resident chunk budget; scissoring draws partial edge tiles and sprites without spilling into the HUD.

`PartyFormation.cs` assigns one walkable cell per non-dead companion. Each leader step resolves all followers together: bounded observed-terrain distance fields guide them toward the previous member's old position, and a small deterministic assignment search prevents duplicate destinations while allowing corridor reversals. Each follower moves zero or one cardinal tile. Recruitment preserves valid nearby cells; stairs and retreat use a connected, radius-six placement query before committing a transfer. A blocked stair landing changes no state. Hostile contact checks the whole group. The client draws individual actor coordinates through per-member `GridMotion`, rather than offsetting every sprite from the leader. Compatible old saves whose entire party is stacked at the leader's cell are expanded deterministically in memory; invalid partial overlaps are rejected and the source save is untouched. Normal formations retain exact save/continued-movement behavior. `--ui-report` includes each member's committed cell and rendered bounds for native acceptance checks.

Backgrounds, terrain sprites and props render in separate batches, allowing texture sorting within each layer as the clear view draws more terrain. Exploration overlays and actors then draw in their original order. Dungeon actors require both their committed and interpolated cells to be visible.

The world backend is graphics-free under `Vagabonds.Core.World`. `WorldPlan.Generate` creates the immutable macro plan; `ChunkGenerator.Generate` creates coordinate-derived terrain and coherently sliced footprints; `WorldSession.Submit` applies validated commands; `AdvanceTicks` advances the deterministic simulation. `IGameSession` and `LegacyCampaignSession` provide save/session dispatch without changing inherited campaign rules.

The surface has 64×64 chunks of 64×64 tiles (4096×4096), with three underground floors. There are 24 towns, six cultures/factions, six biome families and 48 adventure sites. Generation version four seeds and shuffles six biome provinces with smoothly warped borders. Town/site positions vary across the world; a minimum-distance road tree and geographic ring provide alternate routes. Roads cross rivers with bridges. Natural terrain uses global gradient-noise fields. Whole bounded site footprints are generated before slicing, making chunk order irrelevant. Detailed simulation covers a 100-tile radius; terrain prefetch covers 3×3 chunks. The resident terrain cache is capped at 25 chunks, the footprint cache at eight. Persistent edits and discovery are retained separately.

`assets/world/samples.json` contains original structural examples, semantic symbols, culture palettes and explicit rotation/reflection permissions. The overlapping solver learns 3×3 patterns, their frequencies and compatible overlaps. Dynamic bitsets support hundreds of patterns. Validated generation definitions select a layout family per site purpose and floor. Towns/WFC ruins use four varying structural regions around pinned roads/plazas. BSP partitions/room graphs make workings and forts; caverns add coherent noise, and sanctuaries use rings/spokes. Every region has at most eight attempts, followed by a constructive layout if contradictory. Deterioration applies only to ruins; component-connection passes happen afterward. Reports separate learned-pattern output hashes, fallback regions and repaired cells from the final layout. The old socket solver remains unchanged.

The simulation runs at 5 Hz; one real second represents one game minute. Pause, menus/conversations, focus loss and combat choices stop the clock; completed rounds explicitly advance five shared ticks, with no offline progression. New worlds and loaded worlds begin paused. People have stable IDs, generated names, backgrounds, traits, motivations, friends, jobs and homes. The player begins alone and can recruit up to three inhabitants; recruitment removes a worker from production. Nearby people follow work/home destinations and nearby threats pursue the player. Far towns produce and consume stocks; persistent caravans transfer actual cargo along regional routes. Full road blockades trigger rerouting or waiting. Stock shortages affect prices and history. Guard vacancies expose towns to faction raids. Promotion into the detailed radius does not create another actor or stockpile.

The existing four classes and abilities power encounters with one to four party members. World actor IDs are mapped separately from class/party slot and combat unit index. Enemies retain wounds after retreat. Survivors can revive downed companions with medicine. Deliberate abandonment and party wipes record permanent deaths; a bound current save is written immediately. Continuation is blocked while death persistence is pending. A wiped party leaves a recoverable storage cache. The next generated lone traveller inherits the same world, tombstones, history, constructions and economy. A selected final threat from the authored 48-boss pool ends the run when defeated; it exists at a freely accessible depth from creation. Other guardians, seeded loot caches and equipment use the same content catalog. Conditional weighted enemy plans are chosen at round start and saved, with shared ability resolution. The side arc draws from trade, blight or succession premises, references actual sites/towns/patrons, and accepts evidence even if the patron dies. Resolution/failure leaves the world running.

Natural digging yields materials and consumes food according to hardness. Walls, floors, doors, bridges, camps and storage reserve stack resources in timed work orders; workers physically approach and complete them. Partial cancellation or target invalidation refunds once. Owned structures record exact receipts and wear; optional repairs/crafting use the same work system. Dismantling an owned wall, door, empty storage or camp refunds its construction materials without food, avoiding both material duplication and a trap caused by a misplaced wall. Storage transfers preserve quantities. Edits invalidate navigation and cached regional route checks. Local routines use bounded obstacle searches. Stairs link the generated floors; new vertical shafts are unavailable and stairs cannot be built over. Quests check visiting a site, so tunnelling is a valid approach rather than a specific door trigger.

New graphical sessions use `WorldGame`; existing RPG saves dispatch to `VagabondsGame` through `WorldStartup`/`SessionStore`. World input, pause, building, pack, journal, trade, map, combat and inspector use the Core session. The inspector displays original samples and raw/final/repair footprint diagrams. Dedicated world/site/battle/inspector previews never migrate a campaign or autosave unless explicitly saved. The native world smoke reports cache/material/render checks and actual timings. Normal launch resumes existing progress and failed Load attempts leave the current session and pending input intact.

`vagabonds-world` version-three saves pin the content hash, generation version and sample hash. The selected JSON slot is an atomic manifest referencing immutable, SHA-256 checked chunk snapshots in `<slot>.chunks/`. Snapshots are written and flushed before replacing the manifest, so an interrupted save leaves the previous manifest valid. Old snapshots are retained; automatic garbage collection is intentionally deferred so previous manifests/backups stay recoverable. Actors, body state, equipment, memories/trust, clock, reserved work/progress/assignments, structures/condition, incidents, loot/endgame, battle intentions, independent random states, edits, discovery and storage are saved. Incompatible/corrupt world data fails before returning a replacement session. Existing `lantern-rpg` saves retain their original reader and gameplay; field sessions remain rejected.

Research informed the layered design, rather than reproducing Qud's proprietary implementation: [Qud's GDC generation talk](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025913/Math-for-Game-Developers-Tile), [original overlapping WFC](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse), [independent chunk constraints](https://marian42.de/article/infinite-wfc/), [WFC design guidance](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2020/02/08/wave-function-collapse-tips-and-tricks/), and [fixed simulation steps](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/). Solver code and structural samples here are newly written; no Qud art, maps or source code is included.

## Shared bodies and authoring

The same actor body tracks hunger, fatigue, wounds, rest and gathering for party/resident/threat actors. Traits/level/equipment modify common work and combat defaults. Background actors decide when to rest or gather; the party chooses those actions. Large hits/incapacitation create wounds; recovery/treatment works through the shared state. Hunger chips health down to one. Memories are bounded, expire on the shared clock and affect stress/work; trust is directional. Incident conditions derive from actual hunger, fatigue, wounds, work and trust, with independent saved random state/cooldowns. Named residents eat through body rules; regional demand also includes unmodeled population and low-reserve warnings.

Background gathering and distant production remain coarse; individual plants/hauling routes underground are not simulated. Work uses bounded local paths and does not navigate stairs automatically. There is one actor identity across jobs, recruitment, dismissal and combat. No lunar oxygen/suit/colony prerequisites were imported. Generation noise, work reservations, fatigue, social memories and repair concepts were adapted from the pinned Ithica history, with its reference checkout excluded from runtime.

`content/` contains typed definitions for generation, actors, abilities, interactions, bosses/variants, items, recipes, shared simulation and incidents. Stable IDs, reference queries, strict validation, scaffolding and safe removal go through `./dev`; deterministic previews execute Core handlers. `audit-world` outputs JSON/SVG measurements; `verify-native` exercises temporary-slot input under local X11/WSLg. See CONTENT_AUTHORING.md for extension points and VERIFICATION.md for executed evidence.
