# Native migration tuning

These initial values use the numerical-tuning delegation in [the decisions](MIGRATION_DECISIONS.md). They do not change the approved mechanics. The native engine persists `TacticalTuning` with its timeline, so resume cannot silently substitute different recovery or calendar values.

| Value | Initial setting | Meaning and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tactical units per second | 100 | A 100-cost action at speed 100 takes one second. Exact integer recovery still uses the original Roag formula. |
| World calendar conversion | 1,200 tactical units per imported world tick | Existing calendar has 300 ticks/hour; each represents 12 seconds. Fractional world ticks remain in the tactical clock across actions and saves. Sixty ordinary waits advance 60 seconds / five world ticks. |
| Guard recovery cost | 100 | Paid action, scaled by the guarding member's speed. Protection expires when that member next becomes ready; impacts at the same timestamp resolve first. |
| Guard reduction | 50% | Reduces post-defense direct damage from front/front diagonals. Prevented damage rounds down, leaving any odd point as damage. Direct magical attacks qualify; ongoing effects and ground hazards bypass Guard. |
| Wait recovery cost | 100 | Scaled by the actor's speed. Also supplies the interval between a downed member's scheduled bleed-out turns. |
| Revive recovery cost | 100 | Rescuer pays medicine and recovery; rescued member is scheduled with a 100-cost recovery. |

The following are approved fixed mechanics, not open balance choices: five-own-turn bleed-out; damaging hits remove one remaining turn; repeat downing resets five; revival costs one medicine and restores 25% maximum health (integer floor, minimum one); movement costs an action.

Original class costs/stats, RNG, progression thresholds and six-stat growth values remain unchanged. Equipment and existing needs modifiers apply to native actor combat stats; class attack geometry remains class-defined.

Preserved class constants: Duelist's next paid thrust gains +2 after one qualifying parry; Stormcaller caps charge at three and discharges after 100 timeline units; a sentry has three charges, commits first at 100, impacts after 80, and waits 120 after a shot or 200 after a charge without a target. Geomancer's owned wall lasts 300 units. Wayfinder's explored same-room return route is at most six cardinal tiles. These originate in Roag's class sources, not new balance choices. The approved offscreen simulation supersedes original sentry visibility gating and floor-local expiry parking.

World content bindings: ordinary `Floor`, `Earth`, `Grass` and `Bridge` count as plain construct-placement ground; stairs, doors, water, occupied cells and solid fixtures do not. Structural generation supplies original room rectangles; WFC supplies enclosed floor components separated by walls/doors before ruin damage and connectivity repair. Later excavation does not merge authored rooms. Natural outdoor terrain outside authored footprints uses Roag's roomless-map fallback; underground corridors have no room identity. These bindings preserve class eligibility in the larger terrain model rather than making outdoor constructs unusable.

Boon timing/arithmetic preserves original values: temporary speed/wards/priming remain active at the exact expiry timestamp, and refresh keeps the greater unexpired speed/ward rather than stacking it. Wards consume in original catalog order and discard unused capacity after absorbing any part of one hit; later wards remain intact if earlier ones cover the hit. Native damage applies armor/invulnerability, class protection, directional Guard, then boon wards. Ground/ongoing damage bypasses directional Guard but can consume applicable wards. Speed modifies future committed recovery; expiry does not reschedule an action already committed. Proc chance above one million grants whole activations plus one fractional roll; exact whole activations consume no RNG. Reference comparison verifies original half-up coefficient rounding and stream state. Food-related Overflow Chalice conversion remains unresolved and has no implemented adaptation.

Cooperative ability costs, hearing/alarms, food regeneration, extended activity pacing and special-site timing remain to be implemented and documented. The current native engine is under integration; these values are tested in the world-backed engine, not yet a claim of final client balance or rendered acceptance.

Preserved dispatch semantics are not new tuning: the original visits player owners before enemies and subscriptions in catalog order. Native party owners precede other registered participants, with persistent-ID ordering within each group, so world storage and free leader selection do not reorder procs/RNG; a chance roll precedes pair multiplicity, and failed rolls still consume matching unordered pairs. Unique/group conditions mutate their chain history in short-circuit evaluation order, even if a later condition fails. Completing a distinct group resets its count but retains previously seen identities for that chain. Damage history appends only after all subscribers observe the event. Original `pair_adjacent_damage` accepts diagonal adjacency even when authored as `cardinal` because of its existing OR expression; the native resolver preserves that behavior rather than silently changing it during migration. Cooldowns become ready exactly at expiry while existing wards/priming are still active at that timestamp. Visibility queries now take the boon owner to support the approved separate-vision party model.

Native effect execution keeps authored values: ordinary burning ticks every 100 units for three ticks, and refresh retains the committed due time/interval while resetting remaining ticks, keeping stronger power and replacing attribution. Long Wick caps remaining ticks at eight. Sunderer's Mark starts bonus damage at two marks, caps at three and expires after 400 units, inclusively. Hourglass Clasp caps stun at 100 units and applies a 400-unit owner cooldown; it cancels the target's windup but never brings later recovery forward. Temporal Echo resolves after 200 units and Backtrail Mantle after 150, using private locked cells. Ward Ledger spends whole one-hit wards in catalog order even when healing needs only part of their capacity. Mist Lullaby's 400-unit cooldown starts only after actual healing. These are preserved content values, not new balance choices.

Approved party/world extensions: already-burning downed members continue taking their scheduled damaging hits, shortening bleed-out; final death removes their burn. Cross-floor absence does not pause effects. Existing status damage is not stopped by terrain subsequently becoming opaque on the victim's tile. Direct triggered magical/physical effects use directional Guard; ongoing burns bypass it. Healing boons do not revive zero-health members. Purchase refunds credit shared gold and emit a refund, not another currency pickup. Hunger-based food overflow is still undecided and has no chosen implementation.

Attack/payment constants remain original: Pilgrim's Vow costs two HP, is optional on immediate attacks only, must leave one HP and adds its stacked bonus before critical multiplication. Fateful Dice draws one of eight equally likely faces on commitment; previews never draw. The heavy face adds stacked power before criticals. The skull face raises attempted damage to at least current HP plus armor only for eligible regular hostile enemies; Guard, parry and wards can still prevent the kill. The miss face spends action recovery and any selected Vow payment, skips critical RNG and attack/hit procs, and prevents Hookrunner's pull. Nonattack class skills and autonomous discharge/sentry work do not roll. This is native execution behavior, not yet client input/presentation acceptance.

Owned attack modifiers run in original catalog order, preserving composed forms and explicit-aim/bump exceptions. The two dash modifier declarations obtain their behavior through existing movement-event effects (path strike and finite priming). Conditional pattern previews identify hit/kill/dash/wait/beacon-return requirements and delayed timing; they do not promise a proc or a kill. Preview queries use the acting member's sight and omit unseen bodies. Actual execution resolves against current world occupants. Mirror routing and utility/focus behavior remain pending ports.

Native contribution tuning is persisted as `ContributionTuning`: actual enemy HP removed, actual enemy-caused HP healed and effective HP protected each score 1,000 units per HP. Protection is capped to the extra HP that would actually have been lost without it; armor, friendly fire, overhealing and further hits on zero-HP members do not manufacture support credit. Healing consumes outstanding damage attributed to still-unsettled enemies, proportionally by source; debt is removed at enemy death. Current self-wards/Guard/parry credit their owner. Reviving an ally credits the rescuer for the HP actually restored against that debt. Future ally-shield effects must carry the shield owner's identity.

Control scores 40 units per timeline unit: at normal speed, one forced tile or a 100-unit held turn is equivalent to four damage HP. Pulls/pushes count only successful displacement, snares only the actual skipped readiness, and stun only newly added recovery or a canceled windup. Interrupted windups receive one victim movement-recovery equivalent; overlapping delay/cancellation uses the larger value, not their sum. Reapplying stun without interrupting or delaying anything earns nothing. These are delegated starting balance values, not a claim of completed rendered balance testing.

XP uses integer millionths, preserving fractional shares across small kills instead of repeatedly rounding low contributors to zero. Largest remainders allocate the complete reward; persistent member ID breaks exact ties. Only positive contributors participate. Downed members remain eligible; permanently dead members are removed before normalizing weights, so survivors inherit their share proportionally. No eligible contributors means no XP payout. Enemy XP uses the original authored `ExperienceReward` through native enemy registration; untyped actors still default to zero. There is no invented generic reward. Member XP, fractional carry and growth stream are authoritative in `Actor.Tactics`; the legacy prototype XP field remains pending client retirement. Growth applies original thresholds/stat increments and heals living members by max-HP growth; downed members stay at zero HP, and existing recovery timestamps remain committed.

Physical currency and dedicated healing preserve the original collection semantics: kill currency uses the authored amount, explicit health drops restore up to ten HP, and a full-health collector leaves a healing drop on the ground. Pickup itself is part of the committed movement action, not an extra action. Scavenger's Hook gathers at most its power in successful cardinal tiles (N/E/S/W, at most four); all compatible drops on a selected tile are processed in creation order. Carried drops remain unavailable. Funeral Coin adds its power to an existing uncollected currency drop for the credited kill and never directly credits the wallet. Health pickup overflow retains its existing one-hit ward cap/duration. Food supply generation/consumption and its pending overflow question are not decided by this implementation. No new numerical tuning is introduced for currency/healing collection.

The native original-enemy catalog preserves all 50 registered definitions' HP, damage, defense, speed, windup/recovery, movement cost, range and XP/currency values. Enemy critical chance remains zero, matching original spawned enemies. The first native controller group preserves its original attack selection and footprint ordering; enemy attacks use the original default physical damage element. A blocked enemy pays its authored movement cost for Wait (for example the pursuer's 70-cost wait at speed 190 takes 37 timeline units). Pattern counters advance when a windup is committed, including if later canceled. Read-only planning consumes no action time or random draw. These are preserved constants, not new balancing choices. World-role/final-boss scaling, species target policies, navigation and original phase-transition speed changes still require integration; the current controller accepts explicit phase memory rather than silently adding a new trigger.

Authored native enemies commit windup impact timing first, then commit ordinary recovery at impact using their raw speed at that instant. Player boon speed buffs are not part of original enemy timing. A visible pending recovery is a projection, not scheduled readiness; phase/support speed changes before impact can change it. After impact the due time is fixed. Stun cancels windup and respects already-committed recovery; a successful boon shove cancels windup and starts ordinary recovery from the shove time, while a blocked shove leaves it intact. These preserve original timing rather than introducing balance changes.

Expansion enemies preserve original values: Pack Chorister adds one damage per living nearby enemy ally within Manhattan distance two, capped at two; Cinder Mender heals three HP at a locked cell, choosing the lowest HP ratio among allies within three cells with stable ID ties. Forge Conjurer creates a Ruin Skitter at impact with readiness at clock+1, at most three active owned summons and three successful summons over its lifetime; failed occupied landings consume no summon count. Glass Charger locks a two-to-four-cell lane and advances up to three cells after impact, stopping at actual occupied/blocked terrain. Bastion Bulwark primes only after positive hostile damage while surviving, then consumes retaliation on commitment. Summons retain authored XP/currency instead of an invented reward exemption. Barricades break from attack footprints after the primary damage batch; attack destruction does not grant work resources. These are preserved rules, not new numerical balancing decisions.

Squads retain original constants: Ward Weaver protection reaches linked Bodyguard Brutes within Manhattan distance four through a clear line; guards use a six-cell leash. Mend Acolytes heal two HP to their originally selected ally at the locked tile, choosing the lowest health ratio within four cells through a clear line. Banner Captains add one damage to linked followers within five clear cells when their windup commits. Stun or death disables ward/rally support. Shield Bearers block direct hits in the frontal 90-degree wedge, including its diagonal edges; secondary/ongoing damage bypasses the shield, and attacking lowers it throughout windup/recovery until a later non-attack decision raises it. Snare Scribes own at most two traps, choose placement only when at least two untrapped escape cells exist, and each trap inflicts one physical hazard damage plus 40 raw timeline units of delay. Party recovery is extended in place without changing tie order; other enemies pay the delay at their next readiness. These preserve original behavior and are not new balancing choices. Squad population, cross-floor navigation and final presentation remain integration work.

Heavy Rammer retains its authored 110-cost windup and 140-cost recovery at speed 70, and shoves one cardinal tile after impact only if a surviving eligible actor still occupies the locked tile. Shoving moves the victim on the same floor without charging a separate movement action; an evader is unaffected. A blocked shove leaves the victim's pending windup intact; a successful shove starts an interrupted enemy's authored recovery from the shove time. Existing stairs are protected for both enemy and boon shoves.

Hollow Wraith retains its 100-cost phase windup and 120-cost recovery at speed 90. It crosses a single Rock/Wall cell to a legal cardinal destination two cells away that reduces distance to its chosen target. Phase does not deal damage; a later ordinary attack is separate. The locked landing is rechecked at impact, even if the intervening wall was removed. The user explicitly approved offscreen operation under the same physical rules and limits, so selection of the viewed member never controls whether phase is available. This is an approved world adaptation, not a new numerical balance change.

Gulp Toad keeps a four-cell Manhattan loose-loot detection radius, clear-line/occupancy checks and smallest-drop-ID distance ties. Within one cell (including its own tile), it commits a 100-cost gulp windup and 140-cost recovery at speed 80. It carries one drop, rechecks the locked identity/cell at impact and regurgitates that exact amount/identity on death before ordinary kill rewards. Offscreen operation follows the approved world rule. There is no new numerical balancing here; food-drop adaptation remains pending.

Bone Splitter death checks the death tile, north, east, south, west in that order and creates at most two Bone Fragments on free non-stair cells, without carving terrain. New fragments become ready at clock+1, have the authored two HP / one damage / speed 95 / 100-cost windup / 110-cost recovery, and cannot produce XP, loot or further splits. Spawning follows the whole primary damage/removal batch and works offscreen under the approved rule. Future native non-stair exits/reserved boss entries must join protected-landings when those world objects are integrated. The two-body cap and all combat values are original.

Haunted Cache preserves speed 75 and 140-cost bite windup/recovery. An untouched, unalerted cache waits outside cardinal adjacency; adjacency or reduced health activates it, even if movement is blocked. Alerting commits with the action, survives healing and saves, and does not depend on selected-party vision. No new balance constants were introduced.

Grave Cantor preserves speed 80, a 150-cost revival/touch windup and 160-cost recovery. It chooses the nearest eligible corpse within four clear Manhattan cells, using death order for ties. Eligibility storage is capped at 16 markers per floor and 32 total while an unspent living Cantor exists. Committing a revival spends its one lifetime attempt immediately; interruption or blocked landing never refunds it. A successful body becomes ready at clock+1, with half the original maximum HP rounded up, no rewards and no revival chain. Original appearance/identity references are retained without copying ecology roles or status memories. Offscreen operation follows the approved rule. Canceled claimed markers are pruned even with another living unspent Cantor, preventing stale claims rather than inventing additional attempts. No numerical balance changes were introduced.

Alarm Keeper preserves speed 75, a 220-cost call/touch windup and 150-cost recovery. Its one attempt is spent at commitment, even when interrupted or every locked landing becomes blocked. It chooses up to two free cardinal cells in N/E/S/W order; protected stairs and actual world collision exclude landings both when planning and at impact. A blocked tile never retargets. Reinforcements use original Ruin Skitter stats, readiness at clock+1, permanent caller ancestry and reward exemption (no XP, currency, supplies or revival eligibility). The existing retained `spider` atlas mapping supplies their appearance. Offscreen calls use the approved physical-world rule. Original limits and timings are unchanged; final natural encounter placement and rendered presentation remain pending.

Web Weaver preserves speed 90 and 140-cost windup/recovery for both web and bite. It has two lifetime attempts spent on commitment, a five-cell detection range through a clear line, horizontal-then-vertical span preference and eight active webs globally. Each line has anchors four cardinal tiles apart and three interior crossing cells; lines cannot overlap. It lasts 400 timeline units after impact, with no offscreen suspension. Crossing consumes the line and adds exactly 50 raw timeline units to the victim's queued readiness without changing its sequence; without readiness, the victim saves that delay until its next decision. The owner is exempt; other living actors may trigger it, including forced movement and Dash. Damage/RNG are unchanged by crossing. Positive-damage attacks cut reached anchors, using projectile reach captured before primary deaths. Failed/interrupted placement never refunds an attempt; blocked endpoints never retarget. Existing world collision/protected landings replace camera restrictions under the approved rule. No new numerical balance values were introduced.

Oath Twins preserve their original eight HP each and four-cell same-floor bond radius, with clear projectile lines required both ways. Pike retains speed 90, two damage, 100-cost windup and 130-cost recovery; Blade retains speed 100, one damage, 110-cost windup and 130-cost recovery. The bond transfers half of mitigated actual HP loss rounded down, planned from the whole primary batch before any HP changes. Recipients pay no second defense/ward cost; received loss is capped at their pre-batch HP. Sharing never recurses, never adds a synthetic hit and uses zero proc coefficient for shared-only outcomes, while eligible deterministic kill boons and normal rewards remain. Skull bypasses sharing. Separation, blocked lines or death breaks both sides permanently; returning does not reconnect them. Party XP records each member's actual losses on both bodies. Offscreen bonds use physical rules without camera gates. These are preserved mechanics and constants, not new balance values.
