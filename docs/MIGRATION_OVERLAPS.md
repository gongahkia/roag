# Roag / Vagabonds overlap map

Mapping date: 2026-10-26. Initial survey retained as source-analysis evidence. The subsequent active goal authorizes implementation. The [decision record and plan](MIGRATION_DECISIONS.md) resolves many alternatives listed below and takes precedence over this survey's initial open questions.

## Baselines and authority

- Original Roag: `d498fe0889050d2a23f2ff86d80db6084ed6467a`. Its Lua source remains in this checkout unchanged from that revision.
- Original Vagabonds: `38654e90e8546466b2ce9762032155f3890eaf3f`, also available in the sibling repository.
- Current checkout: `eaca280c` before these documentation changes. It contains the imported C# project and preliminary migration work. Prototype behavior is not evidence of an agreed design.
- Accepted direction: handwritten idiomatic C#, MonoGame, product Roag. Adapt overlapping Vagabonds mechanics to Roag; preserve all existing Roag systems/content and retain Vagabonds' world simulation, landscape, tilesets and generic asset catalog.
- Already settled: spatial combat everywhere; direct control of every party member; action-driven time; dungeon actions advance the outside world; rescuable downed individuals; full-party defeat starts a fresh world; persistent terrain-connected dungeons; extraction pressure only in randomly selected special dungeons.

This map covers subsystem responsibilities and semantic conflicts. It is not a claim that every content entry has already received an approved one-to-one conversion or that the prototype passes parity checks.

## Overlaps and adaptation direction

Directions below apply the accepted precedence rule. These are the initial mapping findings: consult the subsequent decision record before treating a detail marked unresolved here as an unanswered question. Remaining uncertainty requires discussion rather than silently inheriting prototype choices.

| Area | Existing systems | Adaptation direction and remaining boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Combat resolution | Roag: tile movement, aim, attack shapes, windups, locked telegraphs, dash, recovery, friendly fire and environmental interactions. Vagabonds: separate battle units, round initiative, mana abilities and staged encounters. | Roag spatial rules govern actual world tiles. Replace the round resolver and battle-stage authority. Preserve compatible Vagabonds ability/enemy concepts by expressing them through Roag actions; exact mana/cooperation conversions remain unresolved. |
| Time | Roag: integer scheduler with effect/impact priority and speed-based recovery. Vagabonds: frame-driven world ticks, round-based combat time and explicit long waits. | One authoritative action-driven timeline feeds both tactics and world simulation. Menus and thinking remain free. Conversion to travel, work, hunger, weather and calendar time needs an explicit scale; fixed time per button press is insufficient. |
| Character identity and party | Roag: one player ID and several global build/class fields; some summons assume a single owner. Vagabonds: persistent people move between resident, worker and party roles; active party limit four. | Keep one persistent person/position/health authority, with Roag combat state attached to that person. Independent control is settled; party size, simultaneous readiness, travel formation and summon ownership limits are not. |
| Classes and talents | Roag: 15 classes with distinct spatial actions, constructs and resources. Vagabonds: Warden, Ranger, Arcanist, Vestal; seeded innate talents and permanent milestone choices. | Preserve all Roag classes. Translate overlapping talents and class concepts into compatible Roag mechanics. Similar names do not imply matching behavior. Placement of non-overlapping Vagabonds abilities and talents needs an explicit content mapping. |
| Stats and levels | Roag: cumulative XP thresholds, randomized growth among six stats, critical chance/damage, speed affecting recovery. Vagabonds: different thresholds, fixed level growth, gear/needs modifying power and initiative speed. | Roag progression/stat semantics take precedence. Re-express equipment and needs against those stats. Decide per-character versus shared progression and XP eligibility, including downed members and kills caused by allies/summons/hazards. |
| Boons and equipment | Roag: stacked rarity-based boons, event triggers, proc chains, transformations, chest choices and one carried utility. Vagabonds: slotted equipment, affixes, signature drops, shared inventory and crafting. | Preserve Roag's boon behavior and utility system; adapt equipment/crafting to the same combat calculations and event pipeline. Resolve ownership, transferability and whether gear may change class-defined attack shapes. |
| Status effects and cooperation | Roag: scheduled effects, shields, traps, movement/control effects and causal event attribution. Vagabonds: guard, stress, marks, burns, stuns and abilities consuming two characters' round turns. | Use Roag scheduling, targeting and event semantics. Map round durations and partner costs to actor recovery; define eligibility, range, resource costs and cancellation before implementing cooperative actions. |
| Loot and economy | Roag: kill-time XP, physical currency/supply drops, paid boon chests and elite rewards. Vagabonds: group-victory receipts, shared supplies, equipment rewards, trade and real cargo. | Preserve Roag pickups/chests and adapt world rewards to them. Keep one-time reward identities across retreat, revival and reload. Decide allocation, prices and physical/shared inventory rules; never pay both kill and encounter rewards for the same entitlement. |
| Food, healing and survival | Roag: food is an immediate healing pickup; health supplies and boon-triggered healing have explicit effects. Vagabonds: food is a finite survival stock; hunger, fatigue, wounds, stress, camping and medicine affect people. | Preserve Roag pickup effects while retaining the world survival simulation. Treat healing food and provisions as distinct concepts until a deliberate conversion is agreed. Wounds/rest must feed the same character state without accidental extra healing or penalties. |
| Downing and death | Roag: single-player defeat and run termination. Vagabonds: rescue, abandonment, persistent deaths and a replacement traveller in the same world after a wipe. | Keep agreed downing/rescue and abandonment; replace same-world continuation with a fresh world after full-party defeat. Clarify rescue range, carrying, resource/time costs and pending hazards when the last member falls. |
| World and dungeon generation | Roag: authored rooms, sockets, corridors, seven biomes, paired floors, stage progression and finale. Vagabonds: large streamed landscape, sites, shared underground floors, structural generation and physical stairs. | Place Roag room/content generation within actual world/site geometry. Retain both content sets and persistent terrain changes. Existing dungeon footprints and floor counts need reconciliation rather than clipping Roag rooms to a fixed battle window. |
| Persistence of places | Roag: floor-local events pause/rebase when inactive, while stage timers continue. Vagabonds: persistent sites, enemies, workers, resources and offscreen simulation. | Places remain persistent and the outside world advances. Unresolved: what happens to dungeon-local enemies, traps, summons and regeneration while absent. A global clock alone does not settle those rules. |
| Extraction and endgame | Roag: stage pressure, deadlines, pursuer, minibosses and Hollow Regent. Vagabonds: contextual guardians, seed-selected final threat and persistent side arcs. | Restrict extraction pressure to the agreed special subset; preserve Roag bosses/stage content in the new world. Decide challenge selection/visibility, activation/reentry, pursuer boundaries, stage-chain placement and what constitutes campaign victory. |
| Terrain and world work | Roag: blocking/vision/projectile geometry, doors, breakables, hazards, ice/bog/bramble/mist and class-created terrain. Vagabonds: digging, building, harvesting, repairs, resource stocks and attributed work. | Use one terrain authority for movement, combat, visibility and work. Preserve Roag terrain effects and Vagabonds costs/consequences. Define which dungeon obstacles can be bypassed through normal excavation/building and how temporary terrain expires. |
| Enemies and encounters | Roag: spatial enemy AI, squads, elites and special mechanics. Vagabonds: persistent contextual groups, wildlife, guardian variants, fixed identities and reward themes. | Preserve Roag enemies and adapt Vagabonds enemies to spatial behavior. Keep population identity and no-duplicate rewards. Encounter size, joining/leaving combat and spatial versions of prepared attacks need design; the old four-enemy battle cap is not an approved new constraint. |
| World society and ecology | Vagabonds: towns, factions, caravans, production, real cargo, occupations, work orders, resident routines, weather, wildlife, relationships, memories, dialogue and quests. Roag contributes combat, terrain and reward events that can affect these systems. | Retain these world systems and drive them from committed simulation time. Connect actual combat/death/terrain events to existing trust, danger, resource and quest consequences. Preserve stable actor identity and attribution through recruitment and dismissal. |
| Profile and saves | Roag: separate achievement/unlock profile, run codec, terminal-save safeguards. Vagabonds: world snapshots, manifests, hashes and validation of persistent identities. | Use native world persistence for integrated state while preserving Roag profile functionality and defeat safeguards. Fresh world versus retained profile progress needs confirmation. Persist scheduled effects, per-person builds, challenge clocks and receipts exactly. |
| Presentation and controls | Roag: keyboard/controller/mouse, aim preview, confirm/cancel, combat feedback, minimap, help, settings, audio and one-bit visual rules. Vagabonds: mouse world UI, party/work/social panels, combat playback and accessibility settings. | Preserve Roag combat readability and controls, adding party selection and world interfaces. Playback must not advance time or repeat resolved events. Decide the visual binding of both art collections; importing assets alone does not resolve style or legibility. |
| Assets and authoring | Roag: Kenney-based visual slots, atlases, themes/audio/world authoring modules and audits. Vagabonds: neutral asset catalog, source archives, Urizen assets, typed content, schema/reference tools, previews and diagnostics. | Keep source art, credits, stable crop IDs and neutral metadata. Bind assets to Roag separately from their generic descriptions. Port Roag authoring capabilities where applicable and adapt native validation/previews to the new rules. Existing catalog provenance is not a second runtime dependency. |

## Discussion status

The subsequent conversation settled party builds, XP eligibility, class/boon ownership, equipment identity, timing, movement, split parties, vision, rescue, targeting, Guard, world persistence, camping, mana/cooperation, talents, dungeon structure, campaign progression and profile retention. Those decisions are recorded in `MIGRATION_DECISIONS.md` rather than retroactively rewriting this initial comparison.

The user's current preference is concise multiple-choice batches of up to five questions where needed. Stop at uncertainty and clarify before engineering the dependent behavior. Do not treat a recommendation or deferred tuning value as approved.

## Implementation constraints for the later plan

- Do not keep parallel combat authorities, duplicate character health, separate unsynchronized clocks or competing reward ledgers.
- Preserve Roag content with an entry-by-entry inventory during implementation; adaptation is not permission to silently drop classes, boons, rooms, interactions, achievements or authoring features.
- Replace single-player globals with explicit ownership. Two people using the same class must not share a sentry, cooldown, progression record or boon state accidentally.
- Preserve deterministic ordering, derived RNG boundaries, preview/commit agreement, free invalid actions and exact save continuation.
- Preserve real cargo/resource accounting and social attribution while connecting combat and world events.
- The generated dynamic-rule runtime and preliminary fixed-size combat window are prototype choices, not the handwritten architecture or approved dungeon design.
- Retire Lua/LÖVE only after native replacements cover the original behavior. Existing translated tests are reference evidence, not automatic proof; native integration and rendered acceptance are also required.
- No builds, gameplay tests, source changes or prototype cleanup were performed as part of this mapping pass.
