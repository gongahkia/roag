# Jōmon-grounded traversal content brief

Status: implementation reference, not archaeological reconstruction. Every effect beyond ordinary material use is Jōmon-inspired fiction.

## Evidence boundary

- [Tokyo National Museum: Daily Tools of the Jōmon Period](https://www.tnm.jp/modules/r_exhibition/index.php?controller=item&id=7867&lang=en) documents stone arrowheads, stone spears, fishhooks, fishing spearheads, harpoon heads, grinding tools, and hammerstones; it is the source for stone-tool silhouettes and wear, not for game abilities.
- [Official Jōmon culture overview](https://jomon-japan.jp/en/learn/jomon-culture) identifies bone and antler implements, bows, arrows, stone spears, fishing, gathering, pottery heated for boiling/storage, chestnut wood for posts, containers, digging sticks, and fuel, plus settlements and ritual items. It supports material and activity vocabulary only.
- [Official Northern Japan prehistoric-sites overview](https://jomon-japan.jp/en/learn/jomon-prehistoric-sites-in-northern-japan) is the source for settlement/site framing. Do not infer a specific tool, rite, or belief from a site name without another cited source.

## Terminology and visual/log constraints

- Use `Jōmon-inspired` for invented mechanics; use `documented` only beside an inline source link.
- Prefer material nouns (`stone`, `antler`, `wood`, `plant fibre`, `clay`, `fire`) over generic exoticism.
- Do not describe a fictional item as “used by Jōmon people” unless that precise use is linked above.
- Do not attribute magic, enemy types, ritual effects, or impossible powers to historical people.
- Keep logs concrete: material, action, visible consequence, then explicit `Jōmon-inspired fiction` where an effect exceeds evidence.
