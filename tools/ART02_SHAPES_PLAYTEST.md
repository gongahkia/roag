# ART-02 Shape-First Presentation Playtest

ROAG intentionally uses procedural shapes for actors. Do not evaluate this as
final art; evaluate whether the shapes make combat easier to read right now.

Run an Expedition and compare all four classes:

1. Gunner — compact circle with a forward barrel.
2. Bruiser — heavy square with an impact wedge.
3. Conductor — diamond with an electrical core.
4. Demolitionist — hexagon with an explosive core.

Fight rushers, skirmishers/ranged enemies, flankers, controllers, heavies, an
elite, and a boss. Exercise projectile shots, melee, electricity, explosions,
and crowded modifier chains.

Record concise notes with these prompts:

- Can I identify a class and its facing without HUD text?
- Can I identify what an enemy does from its shape before it acts?
- Do hostile outlines, threat footprints, and the player attack preview remain
  readable over the glyphs?
- Does the elite double treatment and boss compound shape stand out?
- Are projectiles, electrical arcs, explosions, damage numbers, and chain
  labels readable together?
- Is combat easier to read with clean shapes than with the prior
  placeholder/sprite mixture?
- Does the lack of sprites actually hurt the gameplay experience, or are
  shapes sufficient to understand and enjoy combat?

If a glyph is confusing, record the class/role, terrain, encounter density,
and the exact competing effects on screen. Do not add art assets during this
test; use the findings to tune the shape grammar first.
